# Sri Lanka general statutory pack: guidance

Rules in this pack run from 1 January 2024. The platform computes every figure from the pack's rules; never calculate tax yourself, and never state a rate or amount that the platform has not returned. Checked against the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) website on 2026-11-14.

## When this pack applies

- The business is in Sri Lanka and keeps its books in rupees.
- Two taxes are covered: Value Added Tax (VAT) and the Social Security Contribution Levy (SSCL).
- Not covered: income tax, withholding tax, payroll taxes (APIT, EPF, ETF), customs duties, VAT on financial services, and importer SSCL. If any of these comes up, tell the business the pack does not handle it and that it needs a qualified accountant. See `capability-requests.md`.

Ask first:
1. Is the business registered for VAT?
2. Is the business registered for, and liable to, SSCL?
3. If liable to SSCL: is its turnover mainly services, wholesale or retail, manufacturing, or sales as a registered distributor for a manufacturer?

## Choosing a tax code on a sale

- **VAT-STD (standard-rated):** the default for goods and services supplied in Sri Lanka by a VAT-registered business.
- **VAT-ZERO (zero-rated):** exports of goods, and the specified services that the VAT Act zero-rates, such as certain services to overseas customers. Ask where the customer is, where the service is used, and how payment is received before choosing it. If the business cannot show the conditions, do not use it; suggest the business confirm with its accountant.
- **VAT-EX (exempt):** supplies listed as exempt in the VAT Act schedules. Ask what exactly was supplied. Do not guess: a wrong exemption also removes input tax recovery on related costs.
- A business not registered for VAT charges no VAT, so it picks no VAT code.
- **SSCL codes (one per line, only for liable turnover):** pick the code that matches the business's activity answer. Do not put an SSCL code on a line that is outside SSCL, for example exported goods or a supply the business believes is exempt from the levy. Ask which exemption applies, and keep the answer in the invoice description.
- SSCL is borne by the business. It is not added to the customer's invoice, and the platform records it as an expense and a liability.

## Choosing a tax code on a purchase

- **VAT-STD:** VAT charged by a VAT-registered supplier on a business purchase, where the business can recover it.
- **VAT-NR:** VAT charged but not recoverable by this business, for example where the law blocks the claim. Ask why the claim is blocked before using it.
- **VAT-EX:** the supplier charged no VAT because the supply is exempt.
- Check that the supplier's invoice is a valid tax invoice showing the supplier's VAT registration number. If it is not, ask the business before treating the VAT as recoverable.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the VAT rate for financial services as the standard rate. Financial services follow a different rule that this pack does not cover.
- Adding SSCL to a customer invoice. The pack treats it as the business's own cost.
- Using the same SSCL code for every sale when the business does several activities. The pack takes one activity answer; if a business really mixes activities, say so and ask an accountant to split them.
- Putting VAT into SSCL turnover. SSCL turnover excludes VAT.
- Missing the registration point. Warn a business whose sales are approaching a threshold; the platform supplies the figures and explains them to the business.

## Registration and thresholds

VAT and SSCL each have a registration threshold measured on a quarter and on twelve months. The SSCL thresholds were lowered with effect from 1 July 2026. The platform shows the current figures; explain that crossing a threshold, or being likely to cross it, means the business must apply to register within the time the law allows, and that the platform never registers anyone. Importers and exporters in commerce have their own VAT registration rules.

## Filing calendar

- **VAT:** a return for each tax period is due by the end of the following month; the tax itself is paid earlier, around the twentieth of the following month. The platform shows the dates it knows.
- **SSCL:** returns are filed quarterly; payments are made monthly on a self-assessed basis, around the twentieth of the following month.
- Tell the business that these dates can change by notice and to check the IRD calendar for the year.

## Records to keep

- Valid tax invoices for every sale and purchase, with the customer's or supplier's registration number where required.
- Evidence behind every zero-rated or exempt supply (shipping documents, bank advice for foreign currency, contracts).
- The activity and turnover workings the business used for SSCL.
- Filed returns and payment confirmations.
